\begin{abstract}
LLM inference increasingly accounts for a large share of deployed-AI energy,
and its energy per token changes materially with request shape and serving
configuration.  Selecting a configuration by exhaustive target-GPU trials is
therefore both important and expensive on capacity-constrained HPC clusters.
We present \system{}, a bounded hybrid agent that treats tuning as an
evidence-allocation problem: which configuration should be examined next, at
what fidelity, and when is target-GPU verification worth its cost?  A
constrained LLM planner translates operator intent into typed subgoals, while
deterministic guards select candidate--fidelity actions, enforce GPU-hour and
SLO constraints, maintain the Pareto state, and release only measured
recommendations.  Its evidence substrate, \sandbox{}, combines a CPU-resident
projector, short GPU probes, restricted target execution, immutable provenance,
and explicit abstention.  On one H100 80GB serving Qwen2.5-7B with vLLM, two
preregistered workload-transfer holdouts totaling \BlindMeasurements{} runs
obtain \CalibratedEnergyMAPE{} and \ConfirmationEnergyMAPE{} energy MAPE.  A
separate \ConfigurationCandidates{}-configuration corpus contains
\ConfigurationMeasuredRuns{} balanced L1/L4 measurements.  L0 preserves energy
ranking (Spearman \LzeroEnergySpearman{}) but reverses TTFT ranking
(\LzeroTTFTSpearman{}) and misses the L4 Pareto set.  Short L1 probes reduce
energy, throughput, TPOT, and TTFT MAPE to \LoneEnergyMAPE{},
\LoneThroughputMAPE{}, \LoneTPOTMAPE{}, and \LoneTTFTMAPE{}, recovering the
true frontier with \LoneParetoRecall{} recall before L4 verification.  The
corrected empirical replay reaches the median-L4 oracle in \IPIGOracleHit{} of
\ipig{} episodes versus \RandomOracleHit{} for random while using
\IPIGRandomGPUSaving{} fewer GPU-hours; a cheapest-first ladder matches
\ipig{} at \CheapestIPIGGPUSaving{} lower cost, delimiting the policy claim.  The
verified configuration uses \ParetoEnergyReduction{} less energy, delivers
\ParetoThroughputGain{} higher throughput, and reduces TTFT by
\ParetoTTFTReduction{} relative to the expert default.  These results show why
CPU-first screening is useful, why it cannot be treated as hardware truth, and
how evidence-gated escalation makes agentic inference tuning auditable.
\end{abstract}
