\section{Evaluation Methodology}

The evaluation separates evidence fidelity from acquisition-policy quality.
The first asks whether cheap evidence preserves the decision eventually observed
at L4.  The second asks whether a controller reaches that verified decision
with fewer GPU-hours.  Simulation never defines its own ground truth.

\subsection{Research Questions}

\textbf{RQ1 -- Evidence validity.}  How accurately does the CPU-first model
rank and predict unseen H100 energy, throughput, TTFT, and TPOT as context length
and concurrency change?

\textbf{RQ2 -- Scope reliability.}  Can a preregistered release rule support
predictions where they transfer and abstain where performance fidelity fails?

\textbf{RQ3 -- Frontier recovery.}  Across serving configurations, what does
each evidence level preserve about the L4 SLO-feasible Pareto set, and what
quality can acquisition policies recover per GPU-hour?

\textbf{RQ4 -- Bounded autonomy.}  Does the runtime enforce budgets, preserve
evidence labels, recover from failures, reject malformed planner output, and
withhold recommendations lacking L4 verification?

\subsection{Workload-Transfer Study}

Experiments use one H100 80GB at 700 W and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct with a
digest-pinned vLLM 0.23.0 image.  The transfer study fixes BF16,
TP$=$PP$=$DP$=$1, 256 maximum sequences, 8192 batched tokens, chunked prefill,
and no prefix caching.  Requests use fixed output length, temperature zero, and
ignore EOS.  A cumulative DCGM energy counter brackets only the active benchmark
window; loading, health checks, graph capture, and warmup are excluded.  Every
point has three repeats summarized by median and MAD.

Three anchor repeats use 512 input and 128 output tokens at concurrency eight.
A six-workload development split selects residual correction by
leave-one-workload-out error.  Predictions and a SHA-256 manifest are frozen
before each holdout.  Holdout v2 contains eight workloads and
\HoldoutMeasurements{} runs over 256--2048 input tokens and concurrency 1--32.
Without refitting, scope confirmation v3 contains nine workloads and
\ConfirmationMeasurements{} runs at contexts 384, 768, and 1536 and concurrency
1, 2, and 4.  The validator checks chronology, split, workload, configuration,
power limit, repeat identity, and raw-artifact hashes.  The preregistered primary
gate is energy MAPE $\leq15\%$; TTFT is supported at concurrency four only if
MAPE is $\leq20\%$ and otherwise abstains below four.

\subsection{Frozen Configuration Corpus}

The configuration study fixes the model, GPU, precision, parallelism, 700-W
limit, and a 2048-input/128-output workload at concurrency 32.  Its
\ConfigurationCandidates{} candidates vary maximum sequences
($8,16,32$), maximum batched tokens ($2048,4096,8192$), and chunked prefill;
two no-chunk controls and the operator default
($256$ sequences, $8192$ tokens, chunking enabled) complete the grid.  The SLO
requires P95 TTFT $\leq1600$ ms and P95 TPOT $\leq20$ ms.

All L0 predictions and their manifest are frozen first.  L1 runs 64 requests as
a short probe, whereas L4 runs the preregistered 256-request target workload.
Each candidate--level pair has three repeats in a cyclic-balanced schedule, and
scheduled server restarts isolate configuration changes.  This yields
\ConfigurationLoneRuns{} L1 and \ConfigurationLfourRuns{} L4 runs.  Median L4
records define the exhaustive SLO-feasible oracle.  We report MAPE, Spearman
correlation, pairwise ordering, frontier precision/recall, and realized
GPU-hours.  The full corpus costs \ConfigurationTotalGPUHours{} GPU-h to build;
this offline cost establishes an oracle and is not charged as an online-policy
result.

\subsection{Sealed Policy Replay}

The corpus contains \ConfigurationReplayRows{} rows: 12 frozen L0 predictions
and 72 measured L1/L4 records.  A policy sees a row only after requesting its
candidate--fidelity key, and all policies share the same corpus, seeds, declared
level costs, 0.12-GPU-h online budget, and reserved top-two verification rule.
We preregister 500 seeded, 20-step episodes for the \ipig{} proxy, random,
cost-blind information gain, and cheapest-first acquisition.  Metrics are
verified Pareto precision/recall, regret, GPU-hours to first oracle hit, total
GPU-hours, failed actions, and unnecessary L4 escalations.  The replay runner
constructs its oracle only from sealed L4 records and embeds scenario and corpus
hashes in the report.  Each episode independently bootstraps the
\PolicyHardwareRepeats{} measured repeats for every candidate--fidelity key via
a stable hash of episode seed and action; the mapping is shared across policies
as common random numbers.  Thus the \PolicyEpisodes{} episodes per policy are a
sensitivity analysis over empirical repeats, not \PolicyEpisodes{} independent
hardware experiments.

\subsection{Reliability Tests}

Unit and end-to-end tests inject missing evidence, malformed LLM JSON, launch
failure, budget exhaustion, and SLO-violating verification.  A failed job is
charged its declared upper-bound cost and becomes immutable; it cannot disappear
from accounting.  The LLM selects only a semantic subgoal, while candidate
choice, command routing, budget arithmetic, Pareto filtering, and the final L4
gate remain deterministic.
