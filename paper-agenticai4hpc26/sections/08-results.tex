\section{Results}

\subsection{RQ1: Cheap Evidence Preserves Energy Decisions}

Figure~\ref{fig:energy-parity} shows signed prediction error for all 17 frozen
predictions as a function of unseen measured energy.  On holdout v2, energy MAPE is
\CalibratedEnergyMAPE{}, maximum APE is
\EnergyMaxAPE{}, and rank correlation is \EnergySpearman{}; \EnergyPairwise{}
pairwise orderings (\EnergyPairwisePct{}) are correct.  Median repeat CV is only
\EnergyRepeatCV{}, so model error rather than measurement noise dominates.
Without refitting, scope confirmation obtains \ConfirmationEnergyMAPE{} energy
MAPE, \ConfirmationEnergySpearman{} rank correlation, and
\ConfirmationEnergyPairwise{} correct orderings.  Its largest energy error is
\ConfirmationEnergyMaxAPE{}, passing the preregistered 15\% endpoint on all nine
workloads.  This supports CPU-first energy screening inside the measured
single-H100 envelope, not cross-GPU or multi-node accuracy.

\subsection{RQ2: The Scope Gate Prevents a False Latency Claim}

Aggregate TTFT MAPE in scope confirmation is \ConfirmationTTFTMAPE{}, which
rejects a global claim.  Table~\ref{tab:scope-results} locates the boundary.  At
concurrency four, TTFT MAPE is \KFourTTFTMAPE{} and maximum APE is
\SupportedTTFTMaxAPE{}, passing the 20\% rule.  At concurrency one and two,
TTFT MAPE rises to \KOneTTFTMAPE{} and \KTwoTTFTMAPE{}; the combined sparse
stratum reaches \SparseTTFTMAPE{}.  The generated decision therefore supports
latency at concurrency at least four and abstains below four.  Energy remains
stable across all strata.  The gate narrows the claim instead of turning a good
energy predictor into an unsupported latency recommendation.

\subsection{RQ3: The Probe Recovers What the Sandbox Misses}

All \ConfigurationMeasuredRuns{} preregistered configuration runs succeed;
frozen predictions precede measurements and all \ConfigurationRawArtifacts{}
raw-artifact entries verify.  Table~\ref{tab:configuration-results} shows the
exact verified operating points, while
Fig.~\ref{fig:configuration-fidelity} shows the decision geometry.  The CPU
Sandbox is useful but insufficient: its energy rank correlation is
\LzeroEnergySpearman{} with \LzeroEnergyPairwise{} pairwise accuracy, yet TTFT
correlation is $\LzeroTTFTSpearman{}$.  That inversion changes SLO feasibility,
so the Sandbox frontier selects the expert default and has
\LzeroParetoRecall{} recall of the verified Pareto set.  Releasing that answer would
therefore be wrong despite its informative energy ranking.

The 64-request Probe sharply improves decision fidelity.  Energy,
throughput, TPOT, and TTFT MAPE fall to \LoneEnergyMAPE{},
\LoneThroughputMAPE{}, \LoneTPOTMAPE{}, and \LoneTTFTMAPE{}.  Its provisional
frontier contains three candidates and includes the true verified point, giving
\LoneParetoRecall{} recall and \LoneParetoPrecision{} precision.  The Probe can thus
screen without a false negative, while full verification is still needed to remove two false
positives.

\input{figures/configuration-fidelity}

Among \ConfigurationSLOFeasible{} Verify-feasible candidates, the unique verified
point is \VerifiedParetoConfig{}.  Relative to the preregistered expert default,
it reduces energy by \ParetoEnergyReduction{}, raises throughput by
\ParetoThroughputGain{}, lowers TTFT by \ParetoTTFTReduction{}, and lowers TPOT
by \ParetoTPOTReduction{}.  Average power is slightly higher

finishing the workload sooner rather than suppressing instantaneous power.  The

\ConfigurationLfourGPUHours{} GPU-h for Verify runs; it supplies a sealed oracle for the
predeclared policy replay rather than counting those GPU-hours as agent savings.

\subsubsection{Independent winner confirmation.}
Without refitting or reselection, all \WinnerConfirmationRuns{} new Verify runs
succeed, satisfy both SLOs, and pass artifact checks.  The locked winner uses
less energy in all \WinnerConfirmationPairs{} pairs, saving
\WinnerEnergySaving{} on average (95\% CI [\WinnerEnergyCILower{},
\WinnerEnergyCIUpper{}]; one-sided sign test $p=\WinnerEnergySignTestP{}$),
while median paired TTFT falls \WinnerTTFTReduction{}.  This
\WinnerConfirmationGPUHours{}-GPU-h follow-up independently confirms the
sealed-corpus selection within the same model, workload, and H100.

\subsubsection{Policy replay.}
The left panel of Fig.~\ref{fig:agent-evaluation} reports the corrected
common-random-number bootstrap.  At 0.12 GPU-h, \ipig{} reaches the unique
median verified oracle in
\IPIGOracleHit{} of episodes, \IPIGRandomHitGain{} more often than random, while
using \IPIGRandomGPUSaving{} fewer total GPU-hours.  It preserves cost-blind
information gain's hit rate while using \IPIGCostBlindGPUSaving{} fewer
GPU-hours, isolating the value of cost normalization at this operating point.

The budget sweep sharpens that comparison.  No policy succeeds at 0.04 GPU-h;
at \BudgetThresholdGPUHours{} GPU-h, \ipig{} reaches \IPIGHitAtThreshold{}
success versus \RandomHitAtThreshold{} for random.  Across all five budgets,
their normalized success AUCs are \IPIGSuccessAUC{} and \RandomSuccessAUC{},
respectively.  Cost-blind matches \ipig{} at \CostBlindSuccessAUC{}, whereas
cheapest-first has the highest AUC (\CheapestSuccessAUC{}) and reaches
\CheapestHitAtThreshold{} success at 0.08 GPU-h.

The strongest caveat is equally informative: cheapest-first reproduces
\ipig{}'s verified decisions at \CheapestIPIGGPUSaving{} lower mean cost in
this 12-candidate, single-GPU space.  Moreover, its success falls by
\CheapestBudgetDrop{} after 0.08 GPU-h because additional resampled evidence can
change final verification choices.  The result supports \ipig{} over random and
shows the effect of cost normalization, but neither universal policy superiority
nor monotone anytime behavior.  No replay episode contains a failed action.

\subsection{RQ4: Bounded Runtime Behavior}

The right panel of Fig.~\ref{fig:agent-evaluation} separates LLM proposals
from admitted subgoals.  On V1 development states, raw and
admitted agreement are
\PlannerVoneRaw{} and \PlannerVoneAdmitted{}, with \PlannerVoneFallback{} schema
fallback.  On the disjoint V2 holdout, all outputs are typed and endpoint,
completion, schema-fallback, and forbidden-acceptance counts are zero, but raw
agreement falls to \PlannerVtwoRaw{}.  The model repeatedly chooses
\textsc{Explore} at an SLO boundary or mishandles overlapping scale and SLO
conditions.  Thus it misses the preregistered \PlannerRawThreshold{} criterion.

The state guard intervenes on \PlannerInterventions{} of
\PlannerCallsPerSet{} calls (\PlannerUniqueInterventions{} unique cases, each
repeated three times) and raises admitted agreement to \PlannerVtwoAdmitted{}.
Median/P95 planner latency is
\PlannerVtwoMedianLatency{}/\PlannerVtwoPninetyfiveLatency{} ms.  This is small
relative to multi-second Probe/Verify jobs, but it is measured endpoint latency,
not zero-cost reasoning.  The result validates deterministic semantic admission,
not standalone LLM reliability: the raw gate failed and the guard supplies the
safety property.

All \AutomatedTests{} automated tests pass.  Injected executor failure becomes
a charged immutable record and drives \textsc{Repair}; budget overruns raise a
hard error; and only successful, SLO-feasible Verify evidence can enter the
released recommendation.  Replay and CPU tools cannot relabel simulated output
as verified.
