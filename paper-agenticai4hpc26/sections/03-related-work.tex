\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Energy Measurement, Serving, and Simulation}

Inference-energy studies establish joules/token and joules/request as
first-class metrics and show sensitivity to model, workload, hardware, engine,
decoding, and parallelism
\cite{samsi2023words,fernandez2025energy,niu2025engines}.  MLPerf Power,
ML.ENERGY, and prompt-level datasets add cross-scale measurement or automated
prediction \cite{tschand2024mlperf,chung2025mlenergy,caravaca2025prompts}.
\bench{} contributes declarative, phase-aligned GPU, component, and node
telemetry from single- to multi-node inference \cite{niu2026tokenpowerbench}.
These systems supply observations; they do not allocate a scenario-specific
budget for acquiring the next observation.

Serving systems expose the knob space through memory management, chunked
prefill, disaggregation, scheduling, and heterogeneous placement
\cite{kwon2023vllm,agrawal2024sarathi,zhong2024distserve,
hong2025sola,jiang2025thunderserve}.  Vidur, LLMServingSim, and LLMCompass use
profiling and simulation for what-if analysis
\cite{agrawal2024vidur,cho2024llmservingsim,zhang2024llmcompass}; Lumos validates
distributed trace estimation, Charon combines profiled and analytical operator
backends, and Meta explores millions of configurations
\cite{liang2025lumos,yang2026charon,cho2026deployment}.  These works establish
strong simulator priors.  \system{} instead asks when such a prior is
sufficient and when scale-specific measurement is worth its cost.

\subsection{Energy Control and Multi-Fidelity Optimization}

DynamoLLM and GreenLLM optimize cluster size, parallelism, or frequency under
SLOs \cite{stojkovic2024dynamollm,liu2025greenllm}.  BEAM jointly controls GPU
frequency, prefill chunks, and microbatches using an offline energy--performance
table \cite{lee2026beam}; BOute applies constrained qNEHVI to deployment Pareto
search, and OptiKIT uses full H100 trials for SLO-driven serving tuning
\cite{jiang2026boute,santavas2026optikit}.  They optimize deployment or runtime
actions under a fixed observation model.  \system{} instead optimizes the
evidence-acquisition process before a verified recommendation.

Generic multi-fidelity Pareto optimization is the closest algorithmic
precedent.  $\epsilon$-PAL classifies Pareto membership, PESMO targets Pareto-set
information, and qNEHVI is a strong noisy single-fidelity optimizer
\cite{zuluaga2016epal,hernandezlobato2016pesmo,daulton2021qnehvi}.  MF-OSEMO
selects candidate--fidelity vectors using highest-fidelity frontier information,
while MF-JESMOC adds constraints
\cite{belakaria2020mfosemo,fernandezsanchez2025mfjesmoc}.  We do not claim the
first information-gain or multi-fidelity optimizer.  \ipig{} conditions the
terminal variable on intent-specific SLO feasibility and Pareto membership,
then adds typed evidence stages, guarded execution, and mandatory verified release.

\subsection{Agentic Systems Optimization}

ReAct and Toolformer separate language reasoning from external actions
\cite{yao2023react,schick2023toolformer}.  STELLAR tunes parallel file systems,
LLM-Box uses simulator-guided agents for accelerator mapping, and PROMPTS uses
profiler feedback for multi-agent configuration recommendation
\cite{egersdoerfer2025stellar,pandit2025llmbox,ding2026prompts}.  AIOpsLab shows
that operational agents need controlled workloads, telemetry, fault injection,
and outcome-based evaluation \cite{chen2025aiopslab}.  We therefore test a
narrower hypothesis: semantic planning helps intent translation, repair, and
escalation, while numerical evidence selection remains auditable and is
compared with an optimizer-only controller.

Across these areas, the joint gap is configuration--fidelity co-selection under
real GPU-hour cost followed by full target-workload verification.  No individual
component is claimed as novel in isolation.
