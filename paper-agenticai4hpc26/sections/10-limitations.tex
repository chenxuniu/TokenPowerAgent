\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}

\textbf{Current empirical scope.}  Evidence validation covers one H100, one 7B
model, one vLLM version, one serving configuration, fixed output length, and a
bounded context/concurrency envelope.  It supports workload-transfer and scope
gating, not TP/PP, cross-hardware, or multi-node accuracy.  The configuration
corpus needed for RQ3 is kept visibly unresolved; synthetic replay cannot
support a search-superiority claim.

\textbf{Fidelity and telemetry.}  A single GPU cannot reveal communication,
placement, fabric contention, or scheduler effects.  DCGM cumulative energy
captures GPU-device energy rather than host, network, or facility energy.  It
can also miss short power transients even when total energy remains monotonic.
Every result therefore states its measurement boundary, and multi-node
recommendations require new L2/L3 calibration and independent L4 verification.

\textbf{Uncertainty.}  The frozen intervals are useful release diagnostics but
are not yet calibrated across hardware generations.  New kernels, engines,
GPUs, request processes, or MoE routing may invalidate residual corrections.
The agent can expose distance, uncertainty, and abstention; it cannot make an
unsupported distribution shift disappear.

\textbf{Agent value.}  The implemented planner boundary and safety mechanisms
are tested, but a language model may still choose an unhelpful subgoal.  The
current IPIG estimator is an auditable proxy rather than exact Pareto mutual
information.  Neither mechanism should be called superior until the frozen
configuration corpus shows gains over optimizer-only and non-agent baselines.
