\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}

\textbf{Empirical scope.}  The study covers one H100, one 7B model, one vLLM
version, BF16, fixed output length, and bounded workload/configuration grids.
The measured knob search varies sequence and token limits plus chunked prefill;
it does not establish TP/PP, cross-hardware, MoE, or multi-node accuracy.

\textbf{Fidelity and telemetry.}  A single GPU cannot reveal communication,
placement, fabric contention, or scheduler effects.  DCGM cumulative energy
captures GPU-device rather than host, network, or facility energy.  Multi-node
recommendations therefore require L2/L3 calibration and independent L4
verification.  The L0 TTFT inversion observed here further shows that good
energy ranking must not be generalized to every metric.

\textbf{Uncertainty and policy value.}  Prediction intervals are not calibrated
across hardware generations; new kernels, engines, GPUs, arrival processes, or
MoE routing may invalidate residual corrections.  The current \ipig{} estimator
is an auditable proxy rather than exact Pareto mutual information.  The exhaustive
\ConfigurationTotalGPUHours{}-GPU-h corpus validates fidelity and supplies an
oracle; only the sealed policy replay can attribute GPU-hour savings to the
agent rather than to the surrogate itself.
