\section{Conclusion}

\system{} turns energy-aware inference tuning into a bounded evidence-allocation
loop: interpret intent, choose a configuration and fidelity, observe immutable
evidence, update an SLO-feasible Pareto belief, and verify before recommending.
Across \BlindMeasurements{} blind H100 workload-transfer runs, its CPU-first
model obtains \CalibratedEnergyMAPE{} and \ConfirmationEnergyMAPE{} energy MAPE,
while the release gate exposes a low-concurrency TTFT boundary.  More
importantly, the \ConfigurationMeasuredRuns{}-run configuration corpus shows
why the three stages are necessary: the CPU Sandbox preserves energy ranking
but misses the frontier, the short Probe recovers the true candidate with
\LoneParetoRecall{} recall, and full verification removes false positives.  The
verified configuration improves energy by
\ParetoEnergyReduction{}, throughput by \ParetoThroughputGain{}, and TTFT by
\ParetoTTFTReduction{} over the expert default.  Corrected empirical replay
also places the policy claim precisely: \ipig{} improves oracle-hit rate and
GPU-hour cost over random and cost-blind acquisition, while cheapest-first
remains slightly cheaper in this narrow search space.  These results establish
an auditable CPU-first path from scarce HPC resources to measured,
single-GPU configuration decisions.  Cross-hardware and distributed search are
separate extension questions rather than claims of this paper.
