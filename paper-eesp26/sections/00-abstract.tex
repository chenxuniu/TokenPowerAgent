\begin{abstract}
GPU allocations are scarce on HPC clusters, yet screening every LLM inference
scenario on its target accelerator is costly.  CPU-resident models can explore
workloads without reserving a GPU, but their estimates are not hardware
evidence.  We present \system{}, an evidence-gated, multi-fidelity sandbox that
couples an interpretable CPU projector and sparse measured correction with a
real-GPU executor and freeze-before-measurement validation.  On one H100 80GB
serving Qwen2.5-7B with vLLM, a three-repeat anchor and six development
workloads calibrate transfer across context length and concurrency.  We freeze
eight unseen workloads before collecting three repeats per point.  The
calibrated projector lowers blind energy MAPE from \BaseEnergyMAPE{} to
\CalibratedEnergyMAPE{}, with Spearman $\rho=\EnergySpearman{}$ and
\EnergyPairwisePct{} pairwise accuracy.  Throughput and TPOT MAPE are
\CalibratedThroughputMAPE{} and \CalibratedTPOTMAPE{}; TTFT remains harder at
\CalibratedTTFTMAPE{}, exposing a low-concurrency boundary.  The result supports
CPU-first, GPU-verified screening within a measured envelope, not multi-GPU or
cross-hardware simulation.
\end{abstract}
