\begin{abstract}
GPU allocations are scarce on HPC clusters, yet screening every LLM inference
scenario on its target accelerator is costly.  CPU-resident models can explore
workloads without reserving a GPU, but their estimates are not hardware
evidence.  We present \system{}, an evidence-gated, multi-fidelity sandbox that
couples an interpretable CPU projector and sparse measured correction with a
real-GPU executor and freeze-before-measurement validation.  On one H100 80GB
serving Qwen2.5-7B with vLLM, a three-repeat anchor and six development
workloads calibrate transfer across context length and concurrency.  The same
frozen model is then evaluated on an eight-workload blind holdout and, without
refitting, a nine-workload preregistered scope confirmation---\BlindMeasurements{}
real H100 runs in total.  Energy MAPE is \CalibratedEnergyMAPE{} and
\ConfirmationEnergyMAPE{} in the two studies.  The confirmation passes its
15\% energy endpoint; a preregistered TTFT rule passes at concurrency four
(\KFourTTFTMAPE{} MAPE) but triggers abstention below four
(\SparseTTFTMAPE{}).  Thus CPU-first screening can preserve energy decisions,
while evidence gating detects when performance fidelity does not transfer.
\end{abstract}
