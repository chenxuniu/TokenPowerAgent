\begin{abstract}
LLM inference increasingly dominates the operational energy of deployed AI
services, while workload profiles and serving configurations can substantially
change energy per token.  Energy-aware configuration selection is therefore
important, but profiling every candidate by first deploying it on its target
GPU is impractical when accelerator allocations are scarce and costly.  We
present \system{}, an evidence-gated, multi-fidelity sandbox that couples an
interpretable CPU projector and sparse measured correction with a restricted
real-GPU executor and freeze-before-measurement validation.  On one H100 80GB
serving Qwen2.5-7B with vLLM, a three-repeat anchor and six development
workloads calibrate transfer across context length and concurrency under one
fixed serving configuration.  The same frozen model is then evaluated on an
eight-workload blind holdout and, without refitting, a nine-workload
preregistered scope confirmation---\BlindMeasurements{} real H100 runs in
total.  Energy MAPE is \CalibratedEnergyMAPE{} and
\ConfirmationEnergyMAPE{} in the two studies.  The confirmation passes its
15\% energy endpoint; a preregistered TTFT rule passes at concurrency four
(\KFourTTFTMAPE{} MAPE) but triggers abstention below four
(\SparseTTFTMAPE{}).  Thus CPU-first screening can preserve in-scope energy
decisions while evidence gating detects when performance fidelity does not
transfer, providing a measured foundation for later serving-configuration
search.
\end{abstract}
