\section{From Prediction to Deployment Decisions}

The two holdouts establish workload screening under one fixed configuration, not
configuration selection.  A deployment search must pass three further gates.
\textbf{Ranking} requires top-$k$ recovery, Pareto precision/recall, and
hypervolume error on a frozen candidate set.  \textbf{SLO safety} reports both
the rate of points predicted safe but measured unsafe and whether the final
recommendation is false-safe; intervals must be calibrated before a disjoint
holdout.  Current diagnostic containment is not such a guarantee.
\textbf{Cost} charges every calibration, warmup, failed job, and
verification as
$C_{GPU}(D)=\sum_{e\in D}g_e t_e/3600$ GPU-hours.  CPU screening is reported
separately in CPU-seconds and peak memory: zero target GPU-hours is not zero
energy.  Success requires matched SLO safety with fewer purchased GPU-hours
after disclosing this overhead.  Table~\ref{tab:roadmap} separates completed
evidence from these future claims.
