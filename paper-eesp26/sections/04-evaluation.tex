\section{Evaluation}

\subsection{Platform and Frozen Protocol}

We test workload transfer on one NVIDIA H100 80GB at a 700-W limit (driver
580.173.02, DCGM 4.6.1).  A digest-pinned vLLM 0.23.0 container serves the
pinned Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct revision in BF16.  The fixed server uses
TP$=$PP$=$DP$=$1, 256 maximum sequences, 8192 maximum batched tokens, chunked
prefill, and disabled prefix caching.  Every workload generates 128 output
tokens at unbounded offered rate; request count is eight times concurrency.

The protocol has three chronological splits.  First, \AnchorRepeats{} runs at
512 input tokens and concurrency 8 form the L1 anchor.  Second,
\DevelopmentWorkloads{} development workloads spanning 256--2,048 input tokens
and concurrency 1--32, with three repeats each, fit the residual model.  Its
leave-one-workload-out energy MAPE is \DevelopmentLOOEnergyMAPE{}.  Third, we
freeze predictions for \HoldoutWorkloads{} disjoint context/concurrency pairs,
hash their manifest, and only then collect \HoldoutMeasurements{} L1 runs in
cyclically balanced order.  The validator confirms that predictions precede
measurements and verifies all 62 raw/configuration artifacts.  Base and
corrected values are stored together in each frozen prediction, so the
comparison below gives neither model access to holdout measurements.

\subsection{Blind Holdout Results}

Table~\ref{tab:holdout-validation} reports decision and point accuracy on the
eight unseen workloads.  The sparse correction lowers energy MAPE from
\BaseEnergyMAPE{} to \CalibratedEnergyMAPE{} (a \EnergyMAPEReduction{}
reduction); median and maximum energy APE are \EnergyMedianAPE{} and
\EnergyMaxAPE{}.  Energy rank correlation is $\rho=\EnergySpearman{}$, and
\EnergyPairwise{} workload pairs are correctly ordered.  Both models select
the measured lowest-energy workload, but the base projector badly mis-scales
several intermediate points (Fig.~\ref{fig:holdout-energy-errors}).

\subsection{Error Structure and Abstention}

The point-level values in Table~\ref{tab:holdout-energy-points} span a
21.3$\times$ measured energy range, from 136.8 to 2,918.1 J/1k output tokens.
The corrected model tracks this range rather than regressing toward its anchor.
At concurrency at least 4, post-hoc diagnostic MAPE is 5.64\% for energy,
3.13\% for throughput, 3.14\% for TPOT, and 10.66\% for TTFT.  At concurrency
1, throughput, TPOT, and TTFT MAPE rise to 32.73\%, 22.55\%, and 98.71\%.
This subgroup analysis was not preregistered and is not a second headline
result; it localizes a sparse-occupancy failure for the next scope gate.

For the 2,048/1 point, the model predicts 584.4 W and 4.41 s versus 439.9 W and
6.73 s measured.  Their opposite signed errors yield only 13.77\% energy error,
while TTFT is predicted as 155.9 ms versus 65.3 ms.  We therefore plan to
replace the current axis-aligned envelope with a calibration-distance or
convex-hull gate that abstains near unsupported corners.  The frozen holdout
will not be reused to tune or reevaluate that change.

\subsection{Repeatability, Provenance, and Cost}

Median repeat CV is \EnergyRepeatCV{} for energy,
\ThroughputRepeatCV{} for throughput, \TPOTRepeatCV{} for TPOT, and
\TTFTRepeatCV{} for TTFT.  Frozen intervals contain \DiagnosticCoverage{}
metric--workload medians, but they were not calibrated on an independent set;
we therefore make no statistical coverage claim.  Excluding the three anchor
runs, the 42 development and holdout measurements consumed
\DevelopmentAndHoldoutGPUHours{} H100-hours; the final blind set consumed
\HoldoutGPUHours{} H100-hours.  All 24 holdout records succeeded, cumulative
energy remained monotonic, and each raw trace was covered by the verified
artifact manifest.  The last frozen prediction was created at 17:31:19 UTC;
the first holdout measurement began at 17:37:04 UTC.
