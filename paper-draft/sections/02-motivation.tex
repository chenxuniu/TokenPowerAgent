\section{Motivation and Design Challenges}

\subsection{From Measurement to Decision}

\bench\ already supplies the most important foundation for \system: controlled
inference execution and phase-aligned GPU, node, and system telemetry.  Its
open-source implementation includes single-node engine comparisons and
Ray-based multi-node execution \cite{niu2026tokenpowerbench}.  \system\ does
not replace this measurement path.  It changes how the path is used: prior
runs become calibration data, selected sandbox probes become active evidence,
and final recommendations return to \bench\ for verification.

Figure~\ref{fig:motivation} illustrates the gap.  Configuration choices couple
multiplicatively, while the fidelity required to evaluate them grows
discontinuously when a model crosses GPU, node, or network boundaries.  A
single surrogate over the entire space either wastes measurements in familiar
regions or becomes overconfident in unfamiliar ones.

\subsection{Challenge 1: Phase and Configuration Coupling}

Autoregressive inference alternates between parallel prompt processing and
sequential token generation.  Prefill is commonly compute intensive; decode
often stresses model-weight and KV-cache bandwidth.  Continuous batching,
chunked prefill, prefix reuse, disaggregation, quantization, and power policy
change these phases differently \cite{kwon2023vllm,agrawal2024sarathi,
zhong2024distserve}.  A useful predictor must therefore preserve workload
geometry and the realized serving schedule rather than reduce a request to
model FLOPs or mean GPU utilization.

\subsection{Challenge 2: Cross-Scale Fidelity}

Small probes reveal device-local compute and memory behavior, but not every
large-scale effect.  Within a node, tensor parallelism introduces collective
communication over PCIe, NVLink, or NVSwitch.  Across nodes, fabric bandwidth,
topology, synchronization, pipeline bubbles, and runtime placement become new
energy terms.  Queueing and scheduler interference appear only under traces.
Consequently, the multi-fidelity sandbox must be a \emph{calibration ladder},
not a claim that one GPU can emulate an arbitrary cluster.  Cross-scale
estimates must widen their uncertainty until sparse observations cover the
missing topology.

\subsection{Challenge 3: Spending Measurements Where They Matter}

Uniform sweeps ignore the decision being made.  A candidate far behind the
current Pareto frontier needs no precise estimate, whereas two candidates with
overlapping energy confidence intervals near an SLO boundary may justify a
new run.  The controller must jointly consider predictive uncertainty, Pareto
ambiguity, SLO risk, topology novelty, and measurement cost.  This evidence
allocation decision is the central agentic component of the Pareto search.
