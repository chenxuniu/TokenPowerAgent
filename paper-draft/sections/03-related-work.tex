\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Inference Energy Measurement and Prediction}

From Words to Watts established energy per token and response as first-class
metrics across V100/A100 and multi-GPU LLM inference
\cite{samsi2023words}.  MELODI profiles model, framework, and prompt effects
\cite{husom2024price}; MLPerf Power contributes measurement discipline across
system scales \cite{tschand2024mlperf}; and workload studies show that token
geometry, hardware, software, decoding, and parallelism can change whether an
optimization saves energy \cite{fernandez2025energy}.  A recent engine study
isolates setup and generation energy across Transformers, DeepSpeed,
TensorRT-LLM, and vLLM \cite{niu2025engines}.  \bench\ extends this direction
with declarative configurations, phase alignment, component telemetry, and
single- to multi-node experiments \cite{niu2026tokenpowerbench}.

Recent datasets broaden coverage.  ML.ENERGY measures realistic services and
supports automated recommendations \cite{chung2025mlenergy}; Watt Counts
compares heterogeneous GPU architectures \cite{argerich2026wattcounts}; and
From Prompts to Power learns prompt-level predictors from more than 32,000
measurements \cite{caravaca2025prompts}.  LLMCO2 uses phase and hardware
features for carbon prediction \cite{fu2024llmco2}.  These works establish
valuable ground truth and predictors, but they do not decide which scale of
new evidence is required for a particular Pareto recommendation.

\subsection{Serving Systems and Simulation}

The configuration space is created by serving-system innovations.  vLLM's
PagedAttention improves KV-cache utilization \cite{kwon2023vllm}; Sarathi-Serve
uses chunked prefill \cite{agrawal2024sarathi}; DistServe and Splitwise
separate prefill and decode \cite{zhong2024distserve,patel2023splitwise}; and
Mooncake treats KV cache as a distributed resource \cite{qin2024mooncake}.
These systems optimize throughput, goodput, latency, or cost and expose the
batching, placement, and parallelism knobs that \system\ must reason about.

Vidur profiles operators and simulates end-to-end serving schedules, enabling
large configuration searches without exhaustive execution
\cite{agrawal2024vidur}.  LLM-Pilot profiles hardware to recommend
cost-effective accelerators, while MaverIQ maps intent to deployment choices
through fingerprint profiling and analytical extrapolation
\cite{lazuka2024llmpilot,liakopoulos2025maveriq}.  LLMServingSim and
LLMCompass support large-scale or hardware-aware what-if analysis with
real-system validation \cite{cho2024llmservingsim,zhang2024llmcompass};
Frontier extends simulation toward disaggregated and stateful workloads
\cite{feng2026frontier}.  Simulation-based energy accounting maps schedules to
power and carbon models \cite{ozcan2025simulation}.  Neither
intent-to-configuration nor simulator-guided search is new by itself.  Our
question is how an agent sequentially co-selects a serving configuration and
the scale of evidence needed to verify an energy/SLO decision.

\subsection{Energy-Aware Control and Multi-Fidelity Autotuning}

DynamoLLM jointly configures cluster size, model parallelism, and frequency
under SLOs \cite{stojkovic2024dynamollm}; GreenLLM applies SLO-aware dynamic
frequency scaling \cite{liu2025greenllm}; DualScale models phase-aware
placement and DVFS for disaggregated serving \cite{basit2026dualscale}; and
PALS targets power-aware MoE serving \cite{pals2026}.  The Illusion of Power
Capping cautions that decode can remain below a cap because it is
memory-bound \cite{ma2026illusion}.  These controllers demonstrate real
optimization opportunities but are generally bound to one runtime policy.
Zeus and ytopt provide the broader precedent for measurement-efficient,
surrogate-assisted energy tuning \cite{you2022zeus,wu2023ytoptenergy}.

Multi-fidelity Pareto identification is the closest generic algorithmic
precedent.  $\epsilon$-PAL classifies a finite candidate pool as Pareto,
non-Pareto, or uncertain \cite{zuluaga2016epal}; PESMO targets information
about the Pareto set \cite{hernandezlobato2016pesmo}; and MF-MES normalizes
single-objective information by fidelity cost \cite{takeno2020mfmes}.
MF-OSEMO jointly selects candidate--fidelity vectors using information about
the highest-fidelity Pareto front \cite{belakaria2020mfosemo}, while
MF-JESMOC extends this setting to constraints and multiple fidelities
\cite{fernandezsanchez2025mfjesmoc}.  qNEHVI provides a strong noisy
single-fidelity baseline \cite{daulton2021qnehvi}.  We therefore do not claim
the first information-gain optimizer.  \ipig\ compresses the decision target
to intent-specific SLO feasibility and Pareto membership over typed serving
candidates and operates inside evidence contracts, guarded execution, and
mandatory L4 verification.

\subsection{Agentic HPC}

Tool-using agents such as ReAct and Toolformer separate language reasoning from
external actions, while ToolEmu studies LM-emulated sandboxes before real
execution \cite{yao2023react,schick2023toolformer,ruan2024toolemu}.  In HPC,
Academy coordinates federated scientific agents and LARA executes
validation-driven supercomputer workflows
\cite{kamatar2025academy,dawson2026lara}.  STELLAR tunes parallel file systems,
PROMPTS applies multi-agent profiling feedback to training and serving, and
LLM-Box uses simulator-guided agents for accelerator mapping
\cite{egersdoerfer2025stellar,ding2026prompts,pandit2025llmbox}.  These works
rule out broad claims of a first agentic optimizer.  Our narrower hypothesis is
that semantic planning adds value when an agent must allocate a measured
energy/SLO evidence budget, recover from heterogeneous HPC failures, and leave
all numeric selection and verification to auditable tools.
