\begin{abstract}
Tuning large language model (LLM) inference for energy efficiency requires
searching coupled serving, parallelism, and power settings, yet evaluating one
candidate faithfully may consume an entire multi-GPU node or a multi-node
allocation.  Existing serving controllers, simulators, and Bayesian optimizers
primarily optimize \emph{which configuration} to deploy; they do not jointly
optimize \emph{which fidelity of evidence} to acquire next under a real
GPU-hour budget.  We present \system{}, a bounded hybrid agent that co-selects a
configuration and an evidence level, ranging from historical replay and
simulation to single-GPU probes, sparse scale calibration, and target-cluster
verification.  Its Intent-Conditioned Pareto Information Gain (\ipig{})
criterion values each action by the expected reduction in uncertainty about
the high-fidelity, SLO-feasible energy--performance Pareto set per GPU-hour.
An LLM planner interprets user intent and handles execution failures through
typed tools, while deterministic components enforce feasibility, budget,
Pareto, provenance, and verification constraints.  The current artifact
implements the L0/L1 path with a pinned vLLM server, an isolated benchmark
client, phase-aligned DCGM energy measurement, immutable evidence records, and
a blind-validation gate.  In an initial single-H100 pilot, one three-repeat
Qwen2.5-7B calibration anchor was used to freeze predictions for a workload
with twice the context and concurrency before three holdout measurements were
collected.  Repeat variability was \PilotCVRange{}, point-prediction MAPE over
energy, throughput, P95 TTFT, and P95 TPOT was \PilotMeanMAPE{}, and all
\PilotCoverage{} observed medians fell inside the pre-frozen intervals.  These
results establish end-to-end protocol feasibility, not multi-node fidelity or
Pareto-search superiority; the target H100/H200/B200 campaign is specified but
not yet reported.
\end{abstract}
