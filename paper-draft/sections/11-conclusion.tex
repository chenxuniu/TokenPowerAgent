\section{Conclusion}

\system\ advances \bench\ from post-hoc measurement to agentic multi-fidelity
Pareto search for energy-efficient LLM inference on GPU clusters.  Its central
premise is deliberately modest and testable: a sandbox cannot replace
multi-node measurement, but phase-aware priors, sparse topology calibration,
and uncertainty-guided evidence selection can reduce how many expensive
measurements are needed.  A communication-aware Energy Twin predicts candidate
operating points; a language-model planner chooses typed experiment subgoals;
\ipig\ spends GPU-hours on the configuration--fidelity observations most likely
to change SLO feasibility or the Pareto frontier; and target-scale \bench\ runs
verify every released recommendation.  The resulting workflow connects energy
benchmarking, serving simulation, active autotuning, and bounded agentic HPC
while preserving provenance and reproducibility.  The evaluation tests
cross-scale calibration, Pareto recall per GPU-hour, agent reliability, and
verified energy savings under real latency constraints.
