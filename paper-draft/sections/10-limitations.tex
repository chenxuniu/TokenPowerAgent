\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}

\textbf{A sandbox is not a cluster substitute.}  Single-GPU probes cannot
identify unseen communication, topology, or scheduler effects.  \system\ only
claims measurement reduction, not elimination; multi-node recommendations
require L3 calibration and L4 verification.

\textbf{Telemetry scope and fidelity.}  NVML/DCGM sampling can miss transients,
and summed device power differs from node or facility energy.  Results must
state the telemetry boundary, sampling rate, idle baseline, and meter
calibration.  External meters are used on a representative subset where
available.

\textbf{Generalization.}  New architectures, kernels, engines, fabrics, or MoE
routing can invalidate learned residuals.  Topology distance and interval
coverage expose but do not remove this risk; the correct response is new
calibration, not silent extrapolation.

\textbf{Workload representativeness.}  Public datasets and synthetic traces may
miss production burstiness, prefix sharing, or request cancellation.  Results
will therefore be stratified by token geometry and include at least one
trace-like workload rather than reported as a universal ranking.

\textbf{Agent reliability.}  An LLM may misinterpret intent or choose an
unhelpful tool.  Typed schemas, deterministic validators, budget gates,
provenance checks, and independent verification bound the consequence.  The
evaluation separately reports planning failures instead of hiding them inside
prediction error.
