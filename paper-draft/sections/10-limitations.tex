\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}

\textbf{Current scope.}  The pilot covers one H100, one 7B model, one serving
configuration, and one calibration/holdout pair.  It cannot support Pareto,
cross-hardware, agent, or deployment-benefit claims.  Its \PilotCoverage{}
containment is not calibrated coverage, and \PilotEnergyAPE{} energy error
shows that one anchor is insufficient.

\textbf{Fidelity and telemetry.}  A single GPU cannot reveal communication,
topology, queueing, or scheduler effects; multi-node recommendations require L3
calibration and L4 verification.  DCGM/NVML may miss transients and measures
device rather than node or facility energy, so every claim states its meter
boundary, sampling rate, baseline, and available external-meter calibration.

\textbf{Generalization.}  New GPUs, kernels, engines, fabrics, MoE routing, or
request dynamics can invalidate residuals.  Distance and interval checks expose
but do not remove this risk; unsupported shifts require new evidence.  Any
precision change also retains a fixed output-quality constraint.

\textbf{Agent reliability.}  An LLM may misread intent or choose an unhelpful
subgoal.  Typed schemas, deterministic scores, budget and provenance gates, and
independent verification bound the consequence.  The agent claim remains
unsupported unless it improves intent handling, recovery, or escalation over
the optimizer-only baseline.
