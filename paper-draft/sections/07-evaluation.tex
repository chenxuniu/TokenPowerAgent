\section{Evaluation Methodology and Evidence Status}

The complete evaluation is organized around claims rather than components.  It
first tests whether cheap evidence predicts the target decision, then whether
\ipig{} spends real GPU-hours more efficiently than strong search algorithms,
and only then whether the resulting configuration saves measured energy under
the same SLO.  Agent quality and safety are evaluated separately from optimizer
quality.  At the current artifact snapshot, the end-to-end L0/L1 path and one
blind workload-transfer pilot are complete; L2--L4, Pareto-search, transfer,
and agent-comparison results remain to be collected.

\subsection{Research Questions}

\textbf{RQ1 -- Evidence validity.}  How accurately and calibratably do L0--L3
observations predict unseen L4 energy, tail latency, SLO feasibility, and
Pareto membership across workload, hardware, and node-count shifts?

\textbf{RQ2 -- Search efficiency.}  At equal cumulative GPU-hours, does \ipig{}
recover more of the L4 SLO-feasible frontier than random search, full-fidelity
qNEHVI, generic multi-fidelity MOBO, a fixed calibration ladder, and
simulator-only ranking?

\textbf{RQ3 -- Transfer and scale.}  What evidence can be reused across H100,
H200, and B200 systems, and how many same-hardware L2/L3 probes are required
before target-scale recommendations are reliable?

\textbf{RQ4 -- Agent value and reliability.}  Does the semantic planner improve
search success or recovery over an optimizer-only state machine, and does the
deterministic acquisition policy improve on an LLM-only tool user?

\textbf{RQ5 -- Deployment benefit.}  At matched TTFT/TPOT SLOs, do independent
L4 reruns confirm lower energy than the vLLM default, an expert-tuned
configuration, and the strongest search baseline?

\subsection{Current Single-H100 Blind Pilot}

The pilot uses one H100 80GB at 700 W and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct with a
digest-pinned vLLM 0.23.0 image.  The fixed configuration is BF16,
TP$=$PP$=$DP$=$1, 256 maximum sequences, 8192 batched tokens, chunked prefill,
and no prefix caching.  Random fixed-length requests use temperature zero,
ignore EOS, and arrive at unbounded offered rate.  P95 TTFT and TPOT are
computed per repeat, then summarized with the median and MAD.

Three L1 calibration repeats use 512 input and 128 output tokens, concurrency
8, and 64 requests.  Before holdout execution, the resulting profile predicts
1,024 input and 128 output tokens, concurrency 16, and 128 requests; its file
and manifest are frozen before three L1 repeats.  DCGM device energy is read
around vLLM's main benchmark interval, excluding loading and warmups.  The
validator checks temporal order, split, workload, configuration, power, and
artifact identity.  This pilot tests repeatability, protocol integrity, and
within-GPU workload transfer only.  A separate six-workload, 18-run validation
campaign is frozen but unmeasured; it will calibrate intervals before the final
holdout.

\subsection{Ground Truth and Replay Protocol}

The full study uses two complementary spaces.  In an \emph{oracle subspace}, a bounded grid
is measured densely enough to compute the L4 feasible Pareto set and exact
hypervolume regret.  In a larger \emph{deployment space}, search is not assumed
exhaustive; all returned candidates and a random holdout are measured at L4.
This distinction prevents a simulator or surrogate from defining its own
ground truth.

\bench{} will record synchronized phase timestamps and GPU, CPU/DRAM, and node
telemetry.  Broad calibration points will use at least three post-warmup
repetitions; final L4 comparisons will use at least five matched-trace
repetitions.  Run order is
randomized within blocks of hardware, model, and workload, with temperature,
clock state, and idle baseline recorded.  Device-energy results are integrated
from DCGM/NVML samples; node-meter results are reported separately on the
systems where they are available.

Dense sweeps will be converted into hidden replay environments.  A policy sees
only its selected rows, their realized duration and GPU count, simulator
outputs, and compatible historical records.  Held-out protocols mask (i)
random configurations, (ii) one token/batch region, (iii) one GPU or node
count, (iv) one hardware generation, and (v) a later trace window.  The same
policy is then run through the live ClusterExecutor, and its top candidates are
independently remeasured.  Replay enables repeated policy trials; live runs
test whether replay conclusions survive real execution.

\subsection{Baselines and Ablations}

The completed study will compare three baseline classes.  \emph{Operational
baselines} are the vLLM
default and a documented expert-tuned configuration.  \emph{Search baselines}
are random L4 search, TPE at L4, noisy constrained qNEHVI
\cite{daulton2021qnehvi}, generic multi-fidelity MOBO (MF-OSEMO and
MF-JESMOC) \cite{belakaria2020mfosemo,fernandezsanchez2025mfjesmoc}, a fixed
L0$\rightarrow$L4 ladder, and simulator-only ranking.  An exhaustive grid is
an oracle only in the bounded subspaces.  All adaptive methods receive
identical initial evidence, candidate pools, fidelity costs, random seeds, and
verification reserves.

Mechanism ablations remove, one at a time, topology-aware uncertainty, \ipig{}
in favor of generic variance-per-cost, the LLM planner, failure repair, and the
L4 verification gate.  Agent baselines include an optimizer-only deterministic
state machine and an LLM-only tool user that selects actions from the same
guarded pool.  We also compare cold-start search with H100$\rightarrow$H200,
H200$\rightarrow$B200, and same-hardware cross-node transfer.

\subsection{Metrics and Statistical Method}

Prediction metrics are MAE/MAPE, Spearman rank correlation, interval coverage,
SLO calibration error, and Pareto-membership AUROC.  Decision metrics are
feasible Pareto precision/recall, normalized hypervolume regret, GPU-hours to
95\% of oracle hypervolume, L4 query count, and false-feasible recommendation
rate.  Deployment metrics are joules/token, joules/request, TTFT/TPOT
percentiles, throughput, goodput, and the fixed quality metric for any
precision-changing configuration.  Agent metrics include invalid actions,
recovery rate, unnecessary escalation, provenance violations, planner latency,
and LLM tokens; these costs are reported separately from GPU-hours.

Search results will be paired by scenario, initial evidence, and seed.  We will
report means with 95\% bootstrap confidence intervals, retain failed trials, and use
paired nonparametric tests with effect sizes for headline comparisons.  A
configuration counts as an energy improvement only when its independent L4
run passes the same latency, throughput, and quality constraints as the
baseline.
