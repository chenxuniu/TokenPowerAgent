\section{Problem Formulation}

An inference scenario is a tuple
\begin{equation}
S=\langle M,W,H,R,C,\mathbf o,B_m\rangle,
\end{equation}
where $M$ identifies the model and allowable precisions; $W$ is a request
trace or distribution over arrivals and token lengths; $H$ describes
accelerators, nodes, topology, and power controls; $R$ is the serving-runtime
set; $C$ contains memory, administrative, and service-level constraints;
$\mathbf o$ orders the objectives; and $B_m$ is the real-measurement budget in
GPU-hours.

The candidate space is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal X=\mathcal B\!\times\!\mathcal Q\!\times\!\mathcal P\!\times\!
\mathcal R\!\times\!\mathcal L\!\times\!\mathcal F,
\end{equation}
covering batching and token budgets ($\mathcal B$), quantization
($\mathcal Q$), tensor/pipeline parallelism ($\mathcal P$), runtime policy
($\mathcal R$), placement ($\mathcal L$), and power/frequency policy
($\mathcal F$).  A deterministic compiler produces the feasible pool
$\mathcal X_S$ by removing candidates that violate capacity, parallelism,
runtime support, or site policy.

For $m$ objectives and five evidence levels, let
$\mathbf f^{(\ell)}(x)\in\mathbb R^m$, $\ell\in\{0,\ldots,4\}$, denote the
response at fidelity $\ell$; level 4 is target-scale truth.  The primary
minimization vector is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf f^{(4)}(x)=\langle E_{\rm tok},T_{\rm TTFT},T_{\rm TPOT},-\Theta\rangle,
\end{equation}
where $\Theta$ is throughput.  Let $q_S(x)$ be one exactly when all SLOs in
$C$ hold at L4, and let $\mathbf u\prec\mathbf v$ denote Pareto dominance for
minimization.  The target decision is the feasible nondominated set
\begin{align}
\mathcal X_S^*=\{x\in\mathcal X_S:\;&q_S(x)=1,\ \nexists x'\in\mathcal X_S
\text{ such that}\nonumber\\[-2pt]
&q_S(x')=1\land\mathbf f^{(4)}(x')\prec\mathbf f^{(4)}(x)\}.
\end{align}

At step $t$, the agent has evidence
$\mathcal D_t=\{(x_i,\ell_i,y_i,c_i,z_i)\}_{i=1}^t$, including measured or
simulated outcomes $y_i$, costs $c_i$, and execution status $z_i$.  An action
$a_t=(x_t,\ell_t)$ yields a noisy observation and updates a belief over every
$\mathbf f^{(4)}(x)$.  The agent must return a verified subset
$\widehat{\mathcal X}_S\subseteq\mathcal X_S^*$ while
\begin{equation}
\sum_t c(a_t)\le B_m.
\end{equation}
We evaluate feasible Pareto recall and terminal hypervolume regret as functions
of cumulative real GPU-hours.  This cost axis is essential: a no-GPU replay and
a multi-node target trace are not equivalent queries.
